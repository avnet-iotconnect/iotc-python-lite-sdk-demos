Coffee is a brewed drink made from the roasted and ground seeds, called beans, of the Coffea plant. It is one of the most popular beverages in the world and a major global commodity.

There are two main species of coffee grown commercially: Arabica and Robusta. Arabica is prized for its smoother, more complex flavor and makes up most specialty coffee; Robusta is more bitter, higher in caffeine, and hardier to grow.

Coffee cherries are the fruit of the coffee plant. Each cherry usually contains two seeds, the coffee beans. The beans are green until roasting turns them brown and develops their flavor and aroma.

Roasting transforms green coffee beans with heat. Light roasts keep more of the bean's origin character and acidity, while dark roasts taste bolder and more bitter with roast-driven flavors. The roast level strongly shapes the final cup.

Espresso is a concentrated coffee brewed by forcing hot water under high pressure through finely ground coffee. It forms the base of many drinks and is topped by a layer of golden foam called crema.

A cappuccino is made with roughly equal parts espresso, steamed milk, and milk foam. A latte uses more steamed milk and less foam, giving a milder, creamier drink.

An Americano is espresso diluted with hot water, producing a drink similar in strength to drip coffee but with an espresso character. A macchiato is espresso "stained" with just a small amount of milk.

Caffeine is a natural stimulant in coffee that reduces tiredness and increases alertness by blocking adenosine in the brain. A typical cup of brewed coffee contains roughly 80 to 100 milligrams of caffeine.

The grind size matters greatly: espresso needs a fine grind, drip coffee a medium grind, and methods like French press a coarse grind. The right grind controls how fast water extracts flavor.

The French press, or press pot, brews coffee by steeping coarsely ground beans in hot water and then pressing a metal mesh plunger to separate the grounds, producing a full-bodied cup.

Pour-over brewing pours hot water slowly over coffee grounds in a filter, giving a clean, bright cup and precise control over the brew. Popular pour-over devices include the Hario V60 and the Chemex.

The coffee belt, or bean belt, is the band of tropical regions near the equator where coffee grows best, including countries in Central and South America, Africa, and Southeast Asia.

Brazil is the world's largest producer of coffee by far, followed by Vietnam, which grows mostly Robusta, and Colombia, which is known for high-quality Arabica.

Cold brew is made by steeping coarse coffee grounds in cold or room-temperature water for many hours, producing a smooth, low-acidity concentrate that is diluted and served over ice.

Decaffeinated coffee has had most of its caffeine removed, using water, solvents, or carbon dioxide processes, while aiming to keep the flavor. It still contains a small trace of caffeine.

A barista is a person who prepares and serves espresso-based coffee drinks. Skilled baristas control grind, dose, water temperature, and milk texturing, and may create latte art in the foam.

Fair Trade and other certifications aim to ensure coffee farmers receive better and more stable prices, and to promote sustainable and ethical growing practices.

Coffee is believed to have originated in Ethiopia, according to legend when a goat herder noticed his goats became energetic after eating the berries of a certain plant.

The crema on an espresso is a sign of freshness and proper extraction. It forms from carbon dioxide and oils emulsified under pressure and dissipates within a minute or two.

Single-origin coffee comes from one place, such as a single farm, region, or country, letting drinkers taste flavors distinctive to that origin, while blends mix beans from several sources for a consistent profile.
