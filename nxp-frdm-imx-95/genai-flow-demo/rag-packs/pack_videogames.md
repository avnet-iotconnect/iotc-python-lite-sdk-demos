Video games are electronic games played on devices such as consoles, personal computers, arcade machines, and mobile phones. The industry has grown into one of the largest entertainment sectors in the world.

Pong, released by Atari in 1972, was one of the first commercially successful video games. It was a simple table-tennis game that helped launch the arcade and home console industries.

The video game crash of 1983 nearly destroyed the North American console market due to too many low-quality games. The industry recovered largely thanks to Nintendo's Entertainment System.

The Nintendo Entertainment System, or NES, released in North America in 1985, revived the home console market and introduced enduring franchises. It featured a rectangular controller with a directional pad.

Super Mario Bros., released for the NES in 1985, is one of the most influential games ever made. It defined the side-scrolling platformer genre and made Mario Nintendo's mascot.

Mario is a mustachioed plumber created by Japanese designer Shigeru Miyamoto, who also created The Legend of Zelda and Donkey Kong. Miyamoto is one of the most celebrated game designers in history.

The Legend of Zelda, first released in 1986, pioneered open-world exploration and adventure in games. Its hero is a young character named Link, who rescues Princess Zelda from the villain Ganon.

Pac-Man, released by Namco in 1980, became a cultural phenomenon in arcades. Players guide a yellow character through a maze eating dots while avoiding four colored ghosts.

Tetris, created by Soviet programmer Alexey Pajitnov in 1984, is a puzzle game about arranging falling blocks into complete lines. It became one of the best-selling and most ported games of all time.

Sonic the Hedgehog, introduced by Sega in 1991, was created as a faster rival to Mario and became Sega's mascot, known for high-speed platforming.

The console wars of the 1990s pitted Nintendo's Super NES against Sega's Genesis, driving rapid innovation and famous marketing rivalries between the two companies.

Sony entered the console market with the PlayStation in 1994, using CD-ROM discs that allowed larger games and full-motion video, and it quickly became a market leader.

Doom, released by id Software in 1993, popularized the first-person shooter genre and early online multiplayer. It was widely distributed as shareware.

The Game Boy, released by Nintendo in 1989, was a hugely successful handheld console. Bundled with Tetris, it made portable gaming mainstream and sold tens of millions of units.

Massively multiplayer online games, such as World of Warcraft released in 2004, let millions of players share a persistent online world at the same time.

Minecraft, released fully in 2011, is a sandbox game about placing and breaking blocks to build anything imaginable. It became the best-selling video game of all time.

An arcade game is a coin-operated machine found in public venues. The golden age of arcade games ran from the late 1970s into the early 1980s with hits like Space Invaders, Pac-Man, and Donkey Kong.

A sprite is a two-dimensional image used to represent characters and objects in older games. Early consoles had strict limits on how many sprites and colors they could display at once.

Esports are organized competitive video gaming events, sometimes filling stadiums and offering large prize pools, in games such as League of Legends, Counter-Strike, and Dota 2.

The three major console makers today are Nintendo, Sony with PlayStation, and Microsoft with Xbox, which first launched in 2001 and introduced the Xbox Live online service.
