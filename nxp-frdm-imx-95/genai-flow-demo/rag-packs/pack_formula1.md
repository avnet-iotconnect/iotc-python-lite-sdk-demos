Formula 1 is the highest class of international single-seater auto racing, governed by the FIA (Federation Internationale de l'Automobile). A season runs from March to December across roughly 24 Grand Prix events on tracks around the world.

A Formula 1 race weekend has three practice sessions, a qualifying session that sets the starting grid, and the race itself. Since 2021 some events also run a shorter Sprint race on Saturday that sets its own points and grid.

Points in Formula 1 are awarded to the top ten finishers: 25 for a win, then 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, and 1 for tenth. One extra point is given for the fastest lap if that driver finishes in the top ten.

There are two world championships each season: the Drivers' Championship for the individual driver and the Constructors' Championship for the team. The Constructors' title adds the points scored by both of a team's cars.

Max Verstappen, driving for Red Bull Racing, won four consecutive Drivers' Championships from 2021 through 2024. In 2023 he won a record 19 of the 22 races in a single season.

Lewis Hamilton holds the record for most Formula 1 race wins and shares the record of seven Drivers' Championships with Michael Schumacher. Hamilton moved from Mercedes to Ferrari for the 2025 season.

Ferrari is the oldest and most successful constructor in Formula 1 history, having competed in every season since the championship began in 1950 and winning the most Constructors' Championships.

A modern Formula 1 car is powered by a 1.6-litre turbocharged V6 hybrid engine, called a power unit, that combines an internal combustion engine with electric energy recovery systems. The cars can exceed 350 km/h (about 220 mph).

DRS, the Drag Reduction System, is a movable rear-wing flap a driver can open in designated zones when within one second of the car ahead, reducing drag to make overtaking easier.

Formula 1 tyres are supplied by Pirelli in several dry compounds from hard to soft, plus intermediate and full-wet tyres for rain. Rules usually require a driver to use at least two different dry compounds during a dry race.

The Monaco Grand Prix, run through the streets of Monte Carlo since 1929, is the most prestigious race on the calendar. Its tight, twisting layout makes overtaking extremely difficult and rewards qualifying position.

The Italian Grand Prix at Monza is known as the Temple of Speed for its long straights and low-downforce setups, producing some of the highest average lap speeds of the year.

A pit stop in modern Formula 1 changes all four tyres in around two to three seconds, with a crew of roughly twenty people. Pit strategy — when to stop and which tyres to fit — is often decisive.

The Drivers' Champion is the driver with the most points at the end of the season. If points are tied, the tiebreaker is the number of wins, then the number of second places, and so on.

Ayrton Senna, a three-time champion from Brazil, is regarded as one of the greatest drivers ever, famed for his qualifying speed and wet-weather skill. He died in a crash at Imola in 1994.

The Halo, a curved titanium bar above the cockpit introduced in 2018, protects the driver's head from debris and impacts and is credited with saving several lives.

The safety car is deployed to neutralise the race during hazards, bunching the field together at reduced speed. A Virtual Safety Car instead requires all drivers to slow to a set delta time without physically following a car.

Formula 1 teams operate under a cost cap introduced in 2021 that limits most annual spending, intended to level competition between the largest and smallest teams.

Silverstone in England hosted the very first Formula 1 World Championship race in 1950 and remains the home of the British Grand Prix.

A Grand Chelem, or Grand Slam, is when a single driver takes pole position, leads every lap, wins the race, and sets the fastest lap all in one Grand Prix — a rare feat.
