The National Hockey League (NHL) is the premier professional ice hockey league in the world, made up of 32 teams across the United States and Canada, divided into the Eastern and Western Conferences.

The Stanley Cup is the championship trophy of the NHL and the oldest existing trophy awarded to a professional sports team in North America, first presented in 1893. Winning players get to spend a personal day with the Cup each summer.

An ice hockey game has three periods of twenty minutes each. If the score is tied after regulation in the regular season, teams play a five-minute three-on-three overtime, and if still tied, a shootout decides the winner.

A standard ice hockey team has six players on the ice: three forwards (a center and two wingers), two defensemen, and a goaltender. Teams rotate players in short shifts of roughly 45 seconds.

Wayne Gretzky, nicknamed The Great One, is the NHL's all-time leader in goals, assists, and points. He holds or shares dozens of records and wore number 99, which the league retired for all teams.

Gordie Howe, called Mr. Hockey, played professional hockey across five decades. A "Gordie Howe hat trick" means a player records a goal, an assist, and a fight in the same game.

A hat trick in hockey is when one player scores three goals in a single game. Fans traditionally throw hats onto the ice to celebrate the achievement.

Icing is called when a player shoots the puck from behind the center red line across the opponent's goal line without it being touched, stopping play to prevent teams from simply dumping the puck away.

An offside occurs when an attacking player crosses the opponent's blue line into the offensive zone before the puck does. Play is stopped and a faceoff is held outside the zone.

A power play happens when a team has more skaters on the ice because an opponent is serving a penalty in the penalty box. The team with the advantage is said to be on the power play; the shorthanded team kills the penalty.

The Montreal Canadiens have won the Stanley Cup more times than any other franchise, with 24 championships, and are the oldest continuously operating professional hockey team, founded in 1909.

The Original Six were the NHL teams that made up the league from 1942 to 1967: the Montreal Canadiens, Toronto Maple Leafs, Boston Bruins, Chicago Black Hawks, Detroit Red Wings, and New York Rangers.

The Zamboni is the machine that resurfaces the ice between periods, shaving the top layer and laying down fresh water that freezes into a smooth surface. It is named after inventor Frank Zamboni.

The blue lines divide the rink into three zones: the defensive zone, the neutral zone in the middle, and the offensive zone. The center red line marks the middle of the rink.

A goaltender wears heavily padded equipment including a blocker, a catching glove, leg pads, and a mask. Goalies can stop the puck with any part of their body or equipment.

The Hart Memorial Trophy is awarded to the NHL's Most Valuable Player, while the Art Ross Trophy goes to the player who leads the league in points during the regular season.

International ice hockey is governed by the IIHF, and hockey has been a Winter Olympic sport since 1920. Canada and the Soviet Union / Russia have historically been dominant national teams.

A slap shot is the hardest shot in hockey, where a player winds up and strikes the puck; the fastest recorded slap shots exceed 100 miles per hour.

The Conn Smythe Trophy is awarded to the most valuable player of the Stanley Cup playoffs, and it can be won by a player on the losing team.

Face-offs restart play after a stoppage: two opposing players line up and an official drops the puck between their sticks. Each rink has nine face-off spots.
