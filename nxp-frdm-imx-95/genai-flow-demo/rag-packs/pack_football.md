The National Football League (NFL) is the top professional American football league, made up of 32 teams split into the American Football Conference (AFC) and the National Football Conference (NFC).

The Super Bowl is the NFL's championship game, played each year in early February between the winners of the AFC and NFC. It is consistently the most-watched television broadcast in the United States.

An American football team fields eleven players at a time. The offense tries to advance the ball, the defense tries to stop them, and special teams handle kicking plays like punts and field goals.

A football field is 100 yards long between the goal lines, with a 10-yard end zone at each end, making 120 yards total. The field is 53 and one-third yards wide.

The offense must gain at least 10 yards within four attempts, called downs, to earn a new set of downs. If it fails, possession usually goes to the other team where the ball is spotted.

A touchdown is worth six points and is scored when a team advances the ball into the opponent's end zone. After a touchdown, the team can kick an extra point for one point or attempt a two-point conversion.

A field goal is worth three points and is scored by kicking the ball through the opponent's goalposts. A safety, worth two points, is scored by the defense when an offensive player is tackled in his own end zone.

The quarterback is the leader of the offense, taking the snap and either handing off, passing, or running the ball. Star quarterbacks are usually a team's most important players.

Tom Brady won seven Super Bowls, more than any player in NFL history, most of them with the New England Patriots. He is widely considered the greatest quarterback of all time and retired after the 2022 season.

The Pittsburgh Steelers and New England Patriots share the record for most Super Bowl wins by a franchise, with six each. The Green Bay Packers have the most overall NFL championships including the pre-Super Bowl era.

The Vince Lombardi Trophy is awarded to the Super Bowl champion, named after the legendary Green Bay Packers coach who won the first two Super Bowls.

A first down is achieved when the offense advances the ball at least 10 yards from where its current series began, resetting the count to first down and 10 yards to go.

An interception occurs when a defensive player catches a pass intended for the offense, immediately giving his team possession. A fumble is when a ball carrier loses the ball, which either team can recover.

The line of scrimmage is the imaginary line where the ball is placed before each play. Neither team may cross it until the ball is snapped to begin the play.

A sack is when the defense tackles the quarterback behind the line of scrimmage before he can throw the ball, resulting in a loss of yards for the offense.

The NFL regular season is 17 games per team, followed by a single-elimination playoff of the top seven teams from each conference, culminating in the Super Bowl.

College football is enormously popular in the United States and feeds most players into the NFL through an annual draft, in which teams select eligible players in reverse order of the previous season's standings.

A punt is a kick made on fourth down to give the ball to the opponent as far back as possible, used when a team is unlikely to gain a first down and is too far for a field goal.

The Pro Football Hall of Fame is located in Canton, Ohio, honoring the greatest players, coaches, and contributors in the sport's history.

Overtime in the NFL is a 10-minute period; in the playoffs both teams are now guaranteed a possession, and play continues until a winner is decided.
